Key terms

Parsync · Updated October 2026

Short definitions for owners and operators reading our Insights. These are the ideas behind paring first, then automating in sync with modern AI clients.

AI client

An application where people already work with AI: Cursor, Claude, ChatGPT, and similar tools. The shift for many businesses is not “another AI app,” but running more of the operation where staff and AI share the same screen, files, and connected tools.

Workflow

A repeatable chain of steps: inputs, handoffs, owners, systems, and approvals. Parsync treats one workflow at a time as the unit of improvement, not the whole company at once.

Pare

To cut a workflow back: remove steps that should not exist before you automate or buy another tool. Paring targets duplicate tracking, manual re-entry, and habits that look like work but do not change outcomes. (Rhymes with share, not “parse.”)

Process audit

A structured walk through one workflow end to end: where time and errors pile up, what should be removed, what should stay human, and what is worth connecting to systems or AI.

Context engineering

Designing what an AI client can see, retrieve, and act on safely: the right documents, system data, policies, and boundaries. Good context makes AI useful inside a workflow; missing context keeps it stuck in a chat tab.

MCP (Model Context Protocol)

An open pattern for connecting tools and data to an AI client so the assistant can call real operations (lookup, draft, validate) instead of guessing from a pasted paragraph. It is one path to extensibility without building a separate dashboard for every task.

Integration / extensibility

Systems and services wired into the place people work. Copy-paste between tabs is not integration. The goal is durable connections with clear permissions and auditability.

Human-in-the-loop

Explicit approval steps where stakes are high: clinical judgement, financial decisions, publishing under your brand, or anything regulated. Automation prepares; people confirm.

Automation theater

Automating a broken, duplicate, or pointless process so it fails faster or looks modern without improving outcomes. Often shows up as RPA on top of spreadsheets, or an AI pilot with no system connections.

Sync

After paring, wiring what remains so steps run in order with the right data and handoffs. Sync can include AI clients, integrations, and traditional automation where it still earns its place.
